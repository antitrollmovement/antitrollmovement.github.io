⚡ The Spark

The light that started within.

Don’t erase the hate. Outshine it.

The idea for the AntiTrollMovement came during my own healing journey, after years of surviving what I now understand as cumulative trauma.

The AntiTrollMovement didn’t begin with one comment. It began with an accumulation of moments — the kind you scroll past every day.

Growing up in the foster care system.

Videos that should’ve been innocent and joyful. A mother proudly sharing her twins’ first tastes of food. A family moment that should’ve been safe.

Hiding parts of myself (my sexuality, my sensitivity, my truth) just to fit in.
Enduring bullying that taught me to expect rejection.

But in the comment section, it wasn’t children hiding behind fake names. It was adults — tearing her apart, judging, ridiculing.

And later, finding myself in a relationship that stripped me down until I barely recognised who I was.

And yes, there were kind people too. A few voices saying “ignore the haters” or “you’re doing great.” But the hate was louder.

I've always been proud of my journey, but I've also learned how easy it is to dim your own light when the world keeps telling you to.

I remember feeling angry. My first instinct was to fight. But deep down, I knew it wouldn’t make a difference. Arguing with hate just feeds it.

It wasn't one single event that changed me. It was the moment I finally held a mirror up to myself and asked the hard questions:
Why do I keep shrinking for others? Why do I keep trying to fix people who keep breaking me?

Then it hit me: what if we flipped the script?

And then it hit me, a literal lightbulb moment. 💡

What if, instead of fuelling trolls, we empowered the victims? Not with silence. Not just “delete, block, move on.” But by 🔄 flipping the hate into 💡 light.

I realised trolls will always exist, not just online, but in life.

That was The Spark. The moment AntiTrollMovement was born.

But what shocked me most is that trolls aren't hiding behind fake accounts anymore.
They're professionals.
Teachers.
Carers.
Colleagues.
Everyday people who've become desensitised to cruelty.

Online culture has normalised abuse to the point where empathy now feels like rebellion.

And yet, I know there are people who scroll past hate every day thinking, "What difference could I make?"
Or they wait until tragedy strikes (like the #BeKind movement) and by then, it's too late.

Because the moment to act isn't after the heartbreak.
It's in the comment section before it happens.

That's when I realised:
We don't need to fight trolls, we need to retrain empathy.
We don't need to erase the darkness, we need to outshine it.

That's how the AntiTrollMovement was born.

🔄 What It Means Now

This isn't just a campaign. It's a cultural correction.

We can't change everyone, but we can change what we tolerate.
We can't stop every comment, but we can stop pretending they don't matter.

We don't silence pain, we turn it into purpose.
We don't match hate with hate, we flip it.
We don't wait for platforms to fix it, we become the light ourselves. 💡
